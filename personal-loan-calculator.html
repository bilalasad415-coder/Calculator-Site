How Personal Loan Rates Are Determined

Unlike mortgages or auto loans, personal loans are usually unsecured — not backed by collateral — which means lenders rely heavily on credit score, income, and existing debt to set the rate. This is why personal loan APRs cover a much wider range than secured loans, commonly anywhere from around 7% for excellent credit to well over 30% for borrowers with weaker credit profiles.

Why Origination Fees Matter

Many personal loans deduct an origination fee (often 1-8% of the loan amount) from the funds before disbursing them, meaning the amount that actually lands in your account is less than the amount you're repaying. A loan advertised at a low rate but with a high origination fee can end up costing more than a slightly higher-rate loan with no fee — which is why comparing APR (which factors in fees) rather than the nominal rate alone gives a more accurate comparison.

Common Uses for Personal Loans

Debt consolidation is the most common reason people take out personal loans — combining higher-interest credit card debt into a single fixed-rate, fixed-term loan can lower the overall interest cost and simplify repayment. Other common uses include home improvement, medical expenses, and major purchases where a 0% promotional credit card isn't available or practical.

Fixed vs. Variable Rate Personal Loans

Most personal loans carry a fixed rate, meaning the payment never changes over the loan's life — this predictability is one of the main reasons people choose them over credit cards for large expenses. Variable-rate personal loans exist but are less common, and carry the risk of rising payments if benchmark rates increase during the loan term.
